PHP · SDL · WebGL 2

A 2D game engine, written in PHP.

Load Tiled worlds, build entities from components, and run surface-based platforming in the browser.

Keyboard: arrows / WASD move · Space / Z jump · Down crouches · Tap Jump or hold X while crouching, then release Down to spindash · Hold X through a jump landing to drop dash · Hold C near an airborne ring trail to light-dash · Jump again to glide or fly · Up / Down climb · R reset position · Shift+R restart level · P / Escape pause · F9 debug overlay. Gamepad: left stick / D-pad move and crouch · Tap A / Cross or hold B / Circle while crouching, then release Down to spindash · A / Cross jump · Start / Options pause · Back / Select reset. B / Circle retries downloads in Streaming world. Click the canvas and press a gamepad button to connect. Sticks use a 20% dead zone with proportional movement.

The game pauses in a hidden tab. If the browser releases WebGL, it restores graphics when the context returns and keeps the current game state. Release held gamepad buttons and center the sticks after returning to the game.

Collect rings without tunneling

The Platformer level promotes every animated Tiled tile with the ring class into a PHP entity. Run through one to remove its sprite and update the upper-left player HUD. SCORE, TIME, and RINGS use the same annotated bitmap font as the menus and follow moving-boxes-2's colored-label layout.

Pickup tests sweep the player's full movement path during each fixed update, so a fast actor cannot pass through a ring between frames. Teleports reset that sweep origin. The FPS readout stays separate at the lower left and no longer has a black background. Ring components and setup

Follow ring trails

Choose Level → Light dash with the Runner profile. Jump from the start and hold C or controller X / Square to follow the ring arc. Releasing the button keeps an acquired dash going; the second trail includes a wider gap. Trails work in both directions.

Shift+R or Pause → Restart level restores rings and all other level objects. On a controller, hold B / Circle and press Back / Select. R alone resets the player position. Light-dash components and pickup callbacks

Ride moving platforms

Choose Level → Moving platforms in either menu. Try a floating support that settles under your weight, horizontal and vertical squine shuttles, an elliptical float, and linked paths with square corners. The last gold platform moves only while carrying a rider. Jump off or use Down + Jump to drop through a platform.

Open examples/assets/platforms.tmj in Tiled to edit excursions and connect route points using path and next object references. Components, units, and collision behavior

Enter displaced water regions

Choose Level → Water regions for a pool whose surface is flush with both land banks, or Underwater rail to carry the grinding state through a submerged span and back onto dry ground. Build speed or spindash from a bank to skim. Skimming can only begin while grounded; jumping or falling onto the surface enters the water. Surface crossings emit an animated splash, and a grounded skim retains one directional spray behind the player.

Surface skim rail places two rails directly on the waterline. Each rail overlaps the surface around an open-water gap, producing grinding, skim-grinding, skimming, skim-grinding, and grinding in sequence. Reverse direction to exercise the same transitions backward; the player stays above the surface rather than entering through the water region's side.

Rising water loop rests the loop on the seabed with the initial waterline through its center. Three pressure switches drain, restore, or fill the pool. Jump upward through a plate, then land on its solid one-way top to activate it. Density-based platforms follow the surface, including a 32×160 horizontally locked door block based on object 3958 in pixel-hill-zone. Scripts can activate switches by Tiled object ID and inspect current targets and positions.

The renderer redraws visible water rectangles through a scrolling procedural displacement map. Full-step rectangle crossings preserve entry and exit events when a fast actor crosses the whole volume between updates. Tiled properties, physics state, particles, and rendering setup

Grind a rail and bank a combo

Choose Level → Grinding rail, then hold Right. A short ordinary ground span tests level rail transitions. Four visible quarter bends lift and lower the rail before a separate loop with three collision-layer switches. Keep holding Right into the stop wall, then hold Left to grind the complete course in reverse. Hold Down for the low-friction pose or jump off along the current surface normal.

Tiled collision layers use a grinding property; Rail polylines and Terrain::addRail() cover object and scripted paths. The top-right bitmap HUD shows the live Rail Grind reward, up to four chained actions, overflow, points × multiplier, and the banked or failed result. Full-distance casts prevent fast movement from tunneling through thin rails. Rail, ground transition, quarter-bend, switched-loop, and combo API

Fight a CrabMeat badnik

Choose Level → CrabMeat badniks. Each Tiled object patrols in alternating timed legs, pauses, changes pose, and fires two delayed arcing shots. Walking contact scatters up to sixteen held rings, starts visible mercy flicker, and prevents the damage bounce from counting as an attack. Surviving rings bounce, become recoverable after a short delay, and expire. Roll, jump, or spindash through a badnik to pop it and add 100 points to the HUD. Walking off a ledge remains a plain fall and does not become an attack.

PHP separates the reusable Badnik contact and projectile rules from the CrabMeat schedule. Relative swept contacts catch fast crossings. Projectiles and scattered rings cast their complete movement against terrain, so thin walls remain solid at high speed. Tile classes select animated actor, shot, explosion, and ring art. Components, Tiled properties, and system setup

Defeat an actual boss

Continue into the locked half of CrabMeat badniks to wake the floating mini boss. It tracks the player, drops spike bombs that split when they strike terrain, and takes eight roll or jump hits from the top or sides. An underside hit bounces with no damage. The eighth hit releases the arena; attack the dead shell once more to start its explosion and upward escape.

The reusable Boss component keeps health and phase state separate from one-hit badniks. MiniBosses imports Tiled objects, resolves annotated animation tiles, applies rings, shields, mercy, score, and combo consequences, and exposes encounter state through canvas.dataset.boss. Relative swept contacts cover the boss and fragments, while spike bombs cast their full movement against terrain. Boss components, Tiled properties, and scripting verbs

Break a reward monitor

The CrabMeat level includes animated 10-ring, 30-ring, and five shield monitors. Walk into one or fall onto one after leaving a ledge and it remains a solid obstacle. Roll, jump, or spindash into it to remove the collider during the same continuous movement sweep, reveal a persistent broken casing, launch and fade its screen, rebound in the air, and award the reward once.

MonitorEffect keeps rewards independent from collision and art. Normal and elemental shields absorb a hit without losing rings; electric, fire, and water add distinct movement abilities and immunities. Flight and glide retain an unmodified Jump, while directional Jump selects compatible shield actions during a jump and fire thrust remains additive. Star temporarily overrides and later restores the selected shield. Tiled annotations select screen and aura animations. Shield API and Tiled setup

Inspect a running game

Press F9 with the game focused, or turn on Settings → Debug overlay. The bitmap panel shows position, velocity, grounded state, surface angles, collision layer, camera, entity count, map loading, draw calls, tile uploads, and collision casts per frame. Speeds use pixels per second; angles use radians. World and isometric inspection scenes identify the camera view instead of showing an inactive player.

The unboxed lower-left FPS meter shows browser frames per second and average frame interval. Settings → FPS meter hides it independently. Both preferences survive level changes, and the overlay stays within the game canvas in fullscreen. Diagnostics and scripting

Break a block into sprites

Open the pause menu, then choose Level → Breakable blocks. Walking stops against a block; gain speed and hold Down to roll through it. At rest, hold Down to crouch and look lower after 0.75 seconds. Tap Jump / A / Cross for a discrete charge or hold X / B / Circle to build charge, then release Down to launch. Rolling contact shatters the block and preserves momentum. R or Back / Select restores all blocks. The three blocks split into 4 × 3, 2 × 8, and 8 × 2 grids. Their cropped shards move, spin, and fade while sharing the original sprite atlas.

An intact block has one sprite. After breaking, the same entity owns a SpriteGroup and loses its collision shape immediately. Open examples/assets/breakables.tmj in Tiled to change block sizes and the columns/rows properties. PHP API and lifecycle

Twist tiles and outline sprites

Brake or charge a spindash in a platformer level. The moving-boxes-2-style radial field warps a 64-world-pixel part of the tile backdrop behind the player. The player, particles, HUD, and collision body stay fixed. Reverse direction or charge longer to see the field change strength and direction.

$twist->begin();
$mapRenderer->render($camera, $time);
$twist->compose($camera, $x, $y, 120 * $charge * $direction);
$spriteRenderer->render($sprites, $camera, $time);

Sprites separately get a black outline one world pixel wide. It follows transparent gaps and keeps its world-space thickness when artwork or the camera scales. Breakable boxes and their shards disable it. Backdrop and outline APIs

Stream nearby maps

Open the pause menu, then choose Level → Streaming world. PHP reads the world bounds, preloads nearby maps and their shared assets, and releases distant maps. Use arrows/WASD to pan across the five sections, R to return, and X to retry a failed download. The status line lists ready maps. Watch the Network panel to see when each map downloads.

Anchored backgrounds keep the same world offset as you cross map boundaries. PHP draws one background stack for the map at the camera center. Open examples/assets/stream.world in Tiled to edit the five sections.

Run a loop-de-loop

Choose Level → Loop-de-loop in the pause menu, then hold Right to build speed and run the loop. Hold Left after exiting to run it in reverse. The status line shows the selected collision layer. Losing speed on a wall or ceiling releases the character; jumping pushes away from the current surface.

Open examples/assets/loop.tmj in Tiled. The crown and floor are shared terrain. Layers 1 and 2 hold the two lower halves, and three layer-switch objects select the route. Collision layers do not change drawing order.

Use the PHP engine

require 'autoload.php';

$loader = new PhpWasmEngine\Tiled\Loader;
$map = $loader->map('/assets/level.tmj');
$terrain = PhpWasmEngine\Physics\MapTerrain::build($map);
$movement = new PhpWasmEngine\Physics\Platformer($terrain);
$body = new PhpWasmEngine\Physics\Body(80, 100, 'runner');
